What to share and what to avoid

For an existing issue, tell the electrician which fixture, outlet, switch, or breaker is involved, if you know. Mention any changes you have noticed, such as a device working intermittently or a breaker switching off. Don’t remove covers, handle exposed wiring, or try a repair just to gather more information. If there is smoke, a burning smell, visible sparking, or heat from electrical equipment, move away and call for professional guidance promptly. If there’s an immediate threat, contact emergency services.

For a planned task, prepare a short description of the result you want. If the work is tied to a renovation, have the relevant room or project details handy. A common hiring mistake is assuming the electrician knows what “fix it” means without explaining the symptoms or desired outcome. Another is agreeing to work before you understand what’s included. Ask for a plain explanation of the proposed work, any limits, and the next steps.
